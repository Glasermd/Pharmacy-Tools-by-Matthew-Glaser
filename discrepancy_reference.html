Appendix A: Understanding Billing Discrepancies (SOP vs. Package Insert)

In pharmacy operations, Days of Supply (DOS) calculations must routinely bridge the gap between theoretical clinical math (found in FDA package inserts) and rigid corporate/PBM billing standards designed to prevent audit chargebacks. The calculator tool is hardcoded to reflect billing compliance requirements rather than pure clinical math when discrepancies exist.

Note 1: The Latanoprost Dropper Tip Variance

A classic example of clinical math conflicting with billing SOPs is generic Latanoprost. According to the FDA package insert (which ANDA generics must copy from the brand-name Xalatan), one drop contains approximately 1.5 mcg of latanoprost from a 50 mcg/mL solution. Mathematically, 50 ÷ 1.5 = 33.33 drops per mL.

  • Greenstone (Authorized Generic): Because Greenstone uses the exact same physical bottle as Xalatan, it theoretically dispenses ~33 drops/mL. However, corporate SOP matrices cap this calculation at 20 drops/mL. This protective override accounts for real-world patient administration errors (missing the eye, squeezing too hard) and prevents the patient from running out of medication early and facing a "Refill Too Soon" rejection.
  • Bausch + Lomb / Sandoz (ANDA Generics): These manufacturers utilize distinct plastic dropper tips that physical "drop tests" have proven dispense a smaller drop volume. To maximize cost-savings, PBMs and corporate matrices force pharmacies to calculate these specific NDCs at 36 drops/mL, overriding the text in the manufacturer's own package insert.

Note 2: Topical Exception Limits

Unlike oral medications where quantity directly equals daily usage, topicals (creams, ointments, gels) are highly subjective. To mitigate massive audit liabilities from subjective estimations (e.g., "apply to affected area"), PBMs enforce strict Default Matrices and Exception Limits.

These limits represent the maximum allowable days of supply for a specific package size, regardless of the mathematically derived gram-per-day usage. Submitting a claim that stretches the DOS beyond these fixed exceptions often triggers a "Plan Limit Exceeded" rejection, requiring prior authorization to override.
